Head-to-head vs ocrs 0.12.2 -- tuning corpus, de-duplicated
===========================================================
Measured 2026-09-22. Replaces docs/measurements/2026-09-21_vs_ocrs_660_pages_w16_bank20.txt,
whose corpus and bank both double-counted one font design.

Command
-------
  ./target/release/vs-ocrs bench/pages \
      <pdfcer>/crates/pdfcer-core/assets/models/ocrs \
      16,20,24,32,48 --geometry-weight 16

Corpus: bench/pages/ -- 625 pages, 18 faces x 7 text blocks x 5 render sizes
(14/18/21/28/40 px/em), less 5 pages Inconsolata cannot render because the
currency block contains an em dash and that face has neither dash. (That is an
independent confirmation of the same-day coverage audit, which found
Inconsolata missing U+2013 and U+2014 from its cmap.)

TWO VARIABLES CHANGED AT ONCE against the 660-page run, and the delta below
cannot be attributed to either alone:
  - the CORPUS lost 35 pages (Cascadia Code, a byte-identical twin of
    Cascadia Mono, was being scored twice)
  - the BANK lost that same face, 18,540 prototypes from 20 faces becoming
    17,610 from 19
The second is not neutral: the standardisation constants are per-dimension
moments over the whole bank population, so removing any face re-scales all 107
dimensions for every remaining prototype. See ARCHITECTURE.md section 11,
2026-09-22, duplicate-face entry.

The duplicate was costing OCRcer 0.16 points of margin. The same-day
duplicate-face analysis had PREDICTED about 0.43 points of word-F1 inflation
for OCRcer and about 0.00 for ocrs, from the duplicated face's above-average
score. Measured, it is 0.08 and +0.08. The prediction was the right sign and
roughly three times too large -- recorded because a projection that is not
checked against its measurement is how a projection becomes a fact.

Losses, reported as prominently as the wins
-------------------------------------------
ocrs beats OCRcer on layout-free word F1 in four of seven text blocks, while
OCRcer holds oracle segmentation and oracle spacing:

    drawing      OCRcer 84.67%   ocrs 89.18%    -4.51
    invoice      OCRcer 85.27%   ocrs 91.18%    -5.91
    prose        OCRcer 89.60%   ocrs 91.42%    -1.82
    statement    OCRcer 80.08%   ocrs 86.85%    -6.77

The first of those is the flagship domain. OCRcer reads MORE characters
correctly on the drawing block (96.09% against 95.45%) and still loses the
word metric by 4.51 points, which means its errors are spread across more
distinct words than ocrs's are. That is the shape of a matcher with no
decoder: nothing collapses a near-miss back onto the right word, and every
isolated character error costs a whole word. Chunks 5 and 6 are what address
it, and this figure is the baseline they will be measured against.

OCRcer wins the other three blocks and by larger margins -- technical
+32.78, twins +23.74, currency +11.81 -- which is why the aggregate is +4.02.
An aggregate that is the average of a 33-point win and a 7-point loss is not a
description of an engine that is uniformly better.

By face, ocrs takes three of eighteen on word F1: Inter 88.24 vs 82.75,
Roboto Condensed 84.73 vs 79.25, PT Mono 81.94 vs 81.83 (0.11 -- a tie).
Weakest face for both engines: Open Sans Condensed, OCRcer 61.92% word F1
against ocrs 51.25%.

Against a per-face OCRcer median of 86.86%, the five weakest are Open Sans
Condensed 61.92 (-24.94), STIX Two Math 77.17 (-9.69), Roboto Condensed 79.25
(-7.61), PT Mono 81.83 (-5.03), Inter 82.75 (-4.11). The corpus holds exactly
two condensed faces and they are the first and third weakest. That is a
pattern in two rows and is recorded as a HYPOTHESIS, not a finding; the test
is a per-face error breakdown against set width and stroke width, which has
not been run.

Caveats that travel with every figure here
------------------------------------------
- OCRcer was given every glyph box and every space from the page's own ground
  truth. ocrs ran end to end. This is a matcher measurement, not an engine
  comparison, and the hand-off gate in PLAN.md section 3 requires end to end on
  both sides.
- No decoder, no lexicon, no bigrams, no confusion table. Those are chunks 5-7.
- The corpus is synthetic: hard black and white, unskewed, noise-free, at
  exact integer render sizes. That is the regime this design is strongest in.
- The geometry weight of 16 is a measured candidate, not an authored
  parameter, and it makes 0-against-O substantially worse. ARCHITECTURE.md
  section 4.1.
- bench/pages is the TUNING corpus. The bank's scale ladder was chosen by
  measuring against it, so its figures are corpus-flattered; bench/holdout is
  the same text and faces at five different render sizes and is the better
  estimate.
