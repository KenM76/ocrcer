2026-09-22 — lines.column_gap_heights: the gap populations it sits between

WHAT WAS ASSERTED
  The parameter was authored against a sentence naming three populations, in
  the parameter's own unit (the band's width-weighted median component height):

    "A word space measures about 0.2 median heights and the widest gap inside
     a set line -- a tabbed column in a rendered invoice -- about 1.5, while a
     form's box boundary is several times that."

  None of it was measured. It read as measured, which is what rule 1 forbids.

HOW IT WAS MEASURED
  ./target/release/ocr model/out/ocrcer.ocrw bench/pages-cov --stride 11 \
      --set lines.column_gap_heights=0 --layout

  384 line records over 57 pages, the cut disabled so every band is one uncut
  line, word floor at the shipped 0.3. Every gap classified by the word
  splitter's OWN per-line threshold, which the dump prints — a gap strictly
  wider than the threshold is a space, the rest are intra-word. Classifying by
  a hand-drawn cut instead would have made the answer a function of the cut.

  A first attempt at this table was thrown away. --layout did not honour --set,
  so the dump was post-cut and every family's maximum came out at exactly 2.50.
  See the decision-log entry on the harness bug.

BOTH ASSERTED FIGURES NAMED THE WRONG POPULATION
  "About 0.2" is the INTRA-WORD gap (p50 0.14, p90 0.25-0.31), not the word
  space. A word space is 0.57 at the median on the families without banded
  rows.

  "About 1.5" is the CEILING ON RUNNING TEXT (max 1.25 to 1.64 on four
  families), not the tabbed column gap. The widest gap inside one banded row
  is 3.25 median heights on invoice and 5.30 on statement.

  Both errors understate the separation, which is the harmless direction — the
  populations are further apart than the sentence claimed, not closer. The
  problem is not the conclusion, it is that a reader had no way to know the
  numbers were invented.

THE PREDICTION, AND THE TEST OF IT
  If a family's widest intra-line gap never reaches 2.5, the shipped cut cannot
  fire on it, and the family must score IDENTICALLY with the cut on and off.

  ./target/release/ocr model/out/ocrcer.ocrw bench/pages-cov \
      --only __<family>__ --set lines.column_gap_heights=<0|2.5>

  90 pages per family (85 for currency), all five render sizes:

    family      cut off                          cut at 2.5
    currency    CER 5.615%  F1 82.367%           CER 5.615%  F1 82.367%
    prose       CER 4.155%  F1 89.425%           CER 4.155%  F1 89.425%
    technical   CER 6.357%  F1 77.766%           CER 6.357%  F1 77.766%
    twins       CER 13.164% F1 57.610%           CER 13.164% F1 57.610%
    drawing     CER 4.121%  F1 82.198%           CER 4.528%  F1 82.797%

  Four families identical in every digit of CER, WER, recall, precision and F1.
  Drawing — the one family without banded rows whose tail crosses 2.5, at 8.3%
  of its lines — is the one that moves, and it moves in both directions at
  once: +0.60 points of word F1, -0.41 points of CER.

  So the whole effect of the cut on this corpus at its shipped value lives in
  invoice, statement and the 8.3% tail of drawing. A sweep of the parameter
  pooled over 625 pages is 5/7 dilution at values above about 1.7, and below
  that it starts cutting running text at the word spaces it should be keeping.

CAVEATS
  1. Rendered pages, not scans. A scan's gaps carry the additional spread of
     its own binarisation, and the 29-scan corpus is where the cut and the
     synthetic corpus disagree about whether the cut is worth having at all.
  2. 57 pages at stride 11. Stride 11 is coprime with the 5-size ladder so all
     five render sizes are sampled.
  3. The classification is the splitter's, so it inherits the splitter's
     mistakes: on a badly split line a gap called a space may not be one. That
     is the right inheritance for this question — the cut runs before the
     splitter and the question is what populations the splitter then sees.
  4. This measures the populations the parameter sits between. It does NOT
     choose the value. The value remains a guess; its sweep was invalidated
     when words.min_valley_x_heights changed under it and is being re-run.
