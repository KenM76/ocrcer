WHAT THE SHAPE SAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On the two banded families the cut does exactly what it was built to do and
then costs more than it earns.  Recall rises -- +3.5 on invoice, +7.4 on
statement -- so the cut really is recovering words that a merged row loses.
Precision falls by about fifteen points on both, so it is also manufacturing
words that are not there.  Net word F1 is 3.6 to 5.3 points worse, and CER is
3.7 to 4.3 points worse.

Drawing is the one family where it is a small net win, and the only one without
banded rows whose gap tail crosses 2.5 at all (8.3% of its lines).

**The precision collapse is the thing to explain, and it has not been
measured.** The leading hypothesis is that a short fragment is re-measured on
its own: a four-glyph piece like `Date` has too few members for a reliable
x-height, and every glyph in it is then read against the wrong ruler.  That is
consistent with the cut running before `measure` on purpose -- the reason given
for that ordering is that a band spanning two boxes has no single correct
x-height -- but it means a fragment that is too small to measure gets a
measurement anyway.  The `x_height_source` distribution per fragment length
would settle it and has not been looked at.

Until that is understood, the parameter cannot be chosen honestly: the sweep
optimum would be choosing where a defect hurts least rather than where the
threshold belongs.
