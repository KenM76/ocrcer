Cascadia Code and Cascadia Mono are the same outlines: what that cost, and
what removing one changed

Method
------
Font comparison: Python fontTools, TTFont + RecordingPen, over the 187
classes in model/charset.tsv. Compared per class the full outline draw-command
record (contours and on/off-curve points, in font units) and the horizontal
advance. Outline units, not rasters. Table-level comparison is a byte
comparison of each SFNT table as stored. Both files as installed in
C:\Windows\Fonts on this machine.

Page comparison: cmp(1) over the rendered .pgm pages already on disk in
bench/pages and bench/holdout, produced earlier by `ocrcer-build pages`.

Accuracy: target/release/vs-ocrs over bench/holdout, bank sizes
16,20,24,32,48 px/em, --geometry-weight 16, ocrs 0.12.2 with models read
from D:\Dev\pdfcer\crates\pdfcer-core\assets\models\ocrs (path reference
only; nothing from ocrs enters this project's model file). Runs A and B were
executed back to back in the same session on the same machine from the same
binary; the only difference between them is one row of model/fonts.tsv.

Everything below was measured on 2026-09-22. The OCRcer column throughout is
an oracle-segmentation ceiling, not an end-to-end result -- it is given every
glyph box and every space from each page's own ground truth, while ocrs runs
end to end from the page. The two columns are not like for like.

----------------

    GDEF GPOS HVAR STAT avar cmap cvar cvt fpgm fvar glyf gvar
    hhea hmtx loca maxp post prep gasp
  raw SFNT tables that differ:
    GSUB  (Code's programming ligatures; Mono is the same design with them

2. The rendered pages were byte-identical
-----------------------------------------
  bench/holdout : 35 of 35 cascadia-code pages byte-identical to their
                  cascadia-mono counterpart, 0 differ
  bench/pages   : 35 of 35 byte-identical, 0 differ

  So the per-face table in every benchmark to date had two rows that were
  one measurement printed twice, and 5.3% of every run's wall clock was
  spent reading the same images a second time.


3. What the duplicate cost the reported headline
------------------------------------------------
  The 660-page held-out run recorded on 2026-09-21 is the same corpus with
  the 35 duplicate pages still in it. Run A below is that corpus with the
  duplicates removed and the bank left alone -- so the difference is purely
  the double-counting, with nothing else changed.

                                660 pages (2026-09-21)   625 pages (run A)
    OCRcer char accuracy              95.88%                  95.76%
    OCRcer word accuracy / F1         83.69%                  83.26%
    ocrs   char accuracy              77.74%                  77.72%
    ocrs   word F1                    82.72%                  82.72%

  The duplicate was inflating OCRcer's character accuracy by 0.12 points and
  its word figure by 0.43 points, because the Cascadia design scores 98.00%
  -- well above the corpus mean -- and was being counted twice. It inflated
  ocrs by 0.02 points of character accuracy and nothing measurable on words.
  The error ran in OCRcer's favour, which is the direction that matters in a
  head-to-head.

  Every per-face row in run A is identical to the 660-page run, to the last
  reported digit. Removing duplicate pages changed no face's own score.

5. Why removing a bitwise duplicate changed anything at all
-----------------------------------------------------------
  The matcher is 1-NN over squared L2 on standardised features
  (ocrcer-build/src/bank.rs, Bank::search). Under a fixed metric, deleting a
  prototype that is bitwise identical to one that remains cannot change the
  winner, and cannot change the margin either -- the margin is to the nearest
  prototype of a *different* class, and the removed rows were the same class
  as the rows that stayed.

  So the metric is what changed. The standardisation constants are per-
  dimension mean and standard deviation taken over every prototype in the
  bank (Bank::moments), so face composition sets the scale of every dimension,
  and dropping 5% of the population re-weights all 107 of them slightly.

  That is the whole mechanism. PT Mono lost 1.83 points of word F1 because an
  unrelated face left the bank -- not because anything about PT Mono or its
  prototypes changed.

7. Caveats
----------
- Sections 1 and 5 are proofs from the code and the font files; sections 2,
  3, 4 and 6 are measurements from runs on this machine on this date.
- The accuracy figures are all oracle-segmentation ceilings on OCRcer's side.
- The corpus is rendered from authored text, not scanned pages; nothing here
  speaks to scan noise, blur or skew.
- The -0.03 / -0.11 cost in section 4 is deterministic, not noise. It is an
  artefact of bank composition setting the metric, not evidence that the
  duplicate carried information.


8. Follow-up check: does the distribution change actually hold?
---------------------------------------------------------------
  Verified by running the corpus generator rather than assuming.

  before the fix:
    ocrcer-build pages <dir> 24            -> 18 faces, cascadia-mono only
    ocrcer-build pages <dir> 24 --local    -> 19 faces, cascadia-code AND
                                              cascadia-mono  (WRONG)

  Cause: two implementations of "which faces may this build read".
  bank.rs Fonts::load skipped Excluded unconditionally; main.rs run_pages
  tested `!local && distribution != Shippable`, which under --local skipped
  nothing. Nothing leaked -- the proprietary rows are all status: ineligible
  and FontEntry::file returns None for those -- but the newly-valid
  eligible-present + excluded combination did.

  Fixed by one shared predicate, Distribution::usable(include_local_only),
  called from both sites.

  after the fix:
    ocrcer-build pages <dir> 24 --local    -> 18 faces, cascadia-mono only
    cargo test --workspace                 -> green (97 passed, 11 ignored)
