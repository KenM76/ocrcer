measures. This is reported as a real result, not softened: **on this
pipeline and this corpus, the network classifier at its own best-fit
scale is measurably worse than the prototype matcher, not merely
statistically indistinguishable.** That is the opposite of the
per-glyph internal-val result recorded at chunk 15 integration (net
+3.76 pp overall, driven by 0/O/o), and the gap between the two is
itself the finding — see below.

## Information only, no decision — confusion diff, WER/F1

WER/F1 are in the table above (already reported as part of step 2's
comparison). The confusion diff below re-ran both configurations once
more, `--worst 80`, same 160 pages — a repeat measurement (not the same
run counted for wall time above), informational only.

**0 → O and its neighbours — the net's one clear, corroborated win.**
Prototype matcher (classifier=0): `"0"->"O"` is its **5th**-largest
confusion, 796 occurrences; also `""->"O"` (503, insertion), `"o"->"O"`
(245). None of these three appear anywhere in the net's (classifier=1)
top-80 list at all — under the net, 0/O confusion drops below the
80th-ranked confusion's count (under 109). This directly corroborates the
chunk-15-integration internal-val finding (net 95.58% vs matcher 68.42% on
the 0/O/o group, +27 pp) — it is the one failure mode that demonstrably
carries through the full pipeline into an end-to-end win, even though the
aggregate end-to-end score is a loss.

**i → í/î — not observed either way; a related diacritic confusion is,
and only under the matcher.** Neither `í` (U+00ED) nor `î` (U+00EE) appear
in either classifier's top-80 confusion list. What *does* appear:
`"i"->"ï"` (U+00EF, diaeresis), 204 occurrences, **only under
classifier=0**; absent from classifier=1's list. So the specific risk
named in the architecture entry (an accented-i misread) shows up in this
corpus as a baseline-matcher failure that the net appears to clear, not
one the net introduces.

**Uppercase and digit confusions the net introduces that the matcher's

This is a *cluster* of digit-digit and case confusions (2/8, 0/3, 4/0,
8/6, 3/6, 3/7, 3/2, 9/0, plus C/S, M/C, T/t) that simply is not in the
matcher's top 80 at all, on the same pages. It is exactly the risk the
chunk-15-integration entry named up front ("the net loses on uppercase
and punctuation") — except it is showing here on **finfilings prose**,
which the same entry expected to be comparatively safe for the net
(mostly lowercase), not on a drawing-heavy corpus. The digit confusions
in particular (financial filings carry dense numeric tables) look like
the dominant new failure mode, not the uppercase/punctuation one
originally flagged.

**Net effect on insertions:** the matcher's largest non-whitespace
insertion is `""->","` (541) and `""->"O"` (503); the net's is
`""->"0"` (460) — the net spuriously inserts digit `0` more than the
matcher spuriously inserts any single character.

## What this implies (for `ocrcer-linguist` / `ocrcer-architect`, not decided here)

- The **per-glyph** internal-val comparison (isolated crops, chunk-15
  entry) and the **end-to-end** comparison on the same document class
  disagree in direction: the net's real per-glyph advantage (0/O/o, and a
  smaller general lowercase edge) does not survive segmentation and
  decoding into a net end-to-end win here — it is outweighed by a cluster
  of digit/uppercase/case confusions that the isolated-crop measurement
  did not surface as prominently. That gap is itself worth a dedicated
  look before any further nn.scale or fusion work: something in
  segmentation, decoding, or how digit-heavy tabular context interacts
  with the net's confidence is producing errors the isolated-crop fold
  did not predict.
- This is not a font-coverage or bank-composition question
  (`ocrcer-glyphs`'s territory) — it is squarely inside `match.classifier
  == 1`'s own behavior and how it composes with the decoder
  (`ocrcer-linguist`/`ocrcer-runtime`), consistent with the "Mode 2
  (fused)" escape hatch the chunk-15-integration entry already reserved
  for exactly this shape of result.
- Per the pre-registered rule, the default stays `match.classifier = 0`.
  This was never at risk of flipping without a step-2 pass, and it did
  not get one.
