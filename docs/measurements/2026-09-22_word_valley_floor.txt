2026-09-22 — words.min_valley_x_heights: the floor under a believed valley

WHAT THE PARAMETER IS
  The narrowest gap a believed valley may call a word space, as a fraction of
  the line's x-height. 0 removes the floor and restores the behaviour that
  shipped before this date.

  It is the mirror of words.lone_gap_x_heights. That one is a ceiling on the
  threshold: no valley may sit where a gap this WIDE is called intra-word.
  This one is a floor on the narrowest space: no valley may call a gap this
  NARROW a word space.

  It floors the narrowest gap the valley would call a space, NOT the
  threshold. The threshold sits at the widest INTRA-word gap and is
  legitimately small on well-set text; flooring it breaks correct lines. The
  first implementation floored the threshold and failed the existing unit test
  that a threshold of 1 px is right for gaps of 1, 1, 1, 9, 9.

  A valley below the floor is rejected to words.no_valley_x_heights, the
  no-information rule — not to "this segment has no spaces at all", so a short
  segment that genuinely holds one space still gets it.

WHY IT WAS NEEDED
  A valley search given only intra-word gaps still finds a valley. Otsu
  separability is scale-free: a histogram of 1, 1, 3, 1, 2 px is exactly as
  bimodal as one of 2, 2, 40, 2, 3, so words.min_separability — which exists to
  reject a unimodal line — cannot reject a line that has no word space in it at
  all. The valley is real; the interpretation is not.

  Nothing bound this before, because any line long enough to hold several words
  holds their spaces too. It became live the moment lines.column_gap_heights
  began cutting a banded line into segments that are each a single word. On
  bench/pages-cov invoice pages at 18 px the engine read `Unit` as `U n î t`,
  `Amount` as `A mo u n t`, `112.50` as `112 .50` and `3.75` as `3 .75`.

THE PARAMETER IS A STAIRCASE, NOT A CURVE
  threshold_at truncates, so the value selects an integer pixel floor, and at
  the x-heights in this corpus it is coarse. Computed directly from the shipped
  helper:

    x_height   0.15  0.2  0.25  0.3  0.35  0.4  0.5  0.6
      6.0 px      1    1     1    1     2    2    3    3
      8.0 px      1    1     2    2     2    3    4    4
      9.0 px      1    1     2    2     3    3    4    5
     12.0 px      1    2     3    3     4    4    6    7
     17.0 px      2    3     4    5     5    6    8   10

  So "0.3 against 0.4" is "a 2 px floor against a 3 px floor" on an 18 px page,
  and any two values that truncate alike are the same parameter.

  What that costs, on the four segments that motivated the floor. Valley and
  separability recomputed from the shipped algorithm; the threshold is the last
  gap value belonging to the intra-word class, so a gap strictly greater is a
  space:

    segment (18 px, x-height 9)   gaps           t    eta   narrowest   0.3    0.4
                                                         called space
    112.50 amount cell            1,1,3,1,2      1  0.844       2      reject reject
    Amount header                 1,1,2,1,2      1  1.000       2      reject reject
    Unit header                   1,1,3          1  1.000       3      believe reject
    3.75 amount cell              1,3,1          1  1.000       3      believe reject
    two genuine words             1,1,1,9,9      1  1.000       9      believe believe

  The shipped 0.3 therefore fixes two of the four anecdotes and not the other
  two, while 0.4 fixes all four — and 0.4 measures worse in aggregate at both
  column-gap settings. The extra rejections 0.4 buys cost more elsewhere than
  they gain here. This is why the value was swept rather than argued.

  inert rather than compensating.

  THE SHIPPED VALUE LOSES ON BOTH FAMILIES THE FLOOR WAS BUILT FOR. 0.4 is
  better on invoice by 0.81 points of word F1 and 0.21 of CER, and on statement
  by 2.42 points of F1 and 0.75 of CER. 0.3 is nonetheless the shipped value
  because the pooled corpus is what the engine is graded on and 0.3 wins there
  at both column-gap settings: the other five families lose more under 0.4 than
  invoice and statement gain.

  That is a trade taken deliberately and it is worth restating plainly. The
  parameter was introduced to fix invoice and statement, and the value chosen
  for it is not the value that fixes them best. If the corpus mix is ever
  reweighted toward banded documents — which is the pdfcer use case — this
  choice should be re-swept before it is trusted.

VALUE SWEEP — WHAT CHOSE 0.3
  bench/pages-cov, 90 pages, --stride 7. Stride 7 is coprime with the 5-size
  ladder so every render size is sampled; stride 5 would have selected 14 px
  and nothing else. Two settings of lines.column_gap_heights, because the floor
  and the cut are coupled: the cut is what creates the single-word segments the
  floor exists to protect.

  With the column cut DISABLED (lines.column_gap_heights = 0):

  0.3 is the argmax of word F1 at BOTH settings, and with the cut on it is also
  the CER minimum. The two arms agreeing on the same value is the reason this
  is recorded as measured rather than as the better of two guesses.

  With the cut off, 0.3 costs 0.011 points of CER against the 5.830% minimum
  and buys 0.245 points of word F1. That trade is stated rather than hidden:
  the floor is not free on full-width text.

WHY IT IS NOT 0.4, WHICH WAS THE ARGUED VALUE
  words.no_valley_x_heights is 0.4, and the coherence argument for the floor
  put it AT that value: a valley is believed only if it calls a space the
  no-information rule would also call a space. The argument establishes 0.4 as
  a CEILING — above it the rule contradicts itself, rejecting a valley for
  calling a gap a space and then falling back to a rule that calls the same gap
  a space — and says nothing about where below it the value belongs. The sweep
  says 0.3, on both arms. The argument and the measurement do not conflict; the
  argument was never that precise.

CAVEATS
  1. 90 pages, one sample. The 0.25-to-0.3 difference with the cut off is 0.22
     points of F1 and is inside what a different sample could move. The
     0.3-to-0.4 difference with the cut on is 0.60 points of F1 and 0.14 of
     CER, in the same direction on both arms, which is the difference this
     choice actually rests on.
  2. The 18 px paired isolation was run at 0.4 and has not been repeated at the
     shipped 0.3; the all-sizes per-family arms above have been. The floor-0
     arm of every pair reproduces the pre-change run to the last digit, which
     is what rules out the new code compensating rather than being inert.
  3. lines.column_gap_heights remains a guess. Its own post-floor sweep was
     started at floor 0.4 and stopped when the floor moved; the three values it
     completed (0, 1.0, 1.5 on all 625 pages, at floor 0.4) are superseded and
     are not reported here as a choice. What they did show is that the floor
     rehabilitates the cut: at 1.5 the cut scored 67.160% word F1 before the
     floor and 74.665% after, against 75.020% with the cut off.
  4. The synthetic corpus and the 29 real scans disagree about the cut. The
     cut is a net loss on bench/pages-cov at every value tried and worth
     33.81% -> 43.40% on the third-party harness. That disagreement is open.
  5. The 43.40% third-party figure predates the floor and has not been re-run.
