## Compared with the pre-L1 numbers (`2026-09-26_c15_measure.md`)

| | pre-L1 e2e / line-matched | post-L1 e2e / line-matched | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| classifier=0 | 19.954 / 21.660 | 19.933 / 21.617 | −0.021 / −0.043 pp |
| classifier=1@√2 | 21.048 / 23.896 | 21.026 / 23.825 | −0.022 / −0.071 pp |
| **Δ (1−0)** | +1.094 / +2.236 pp | +1.093 / +2.208 pp | −0.001 / −0.028 pp |

Both classifiers improved by almost exactly the amount L1's own val
measurement reported (−0.032 / −0.042 pp on the 103-page val set), so this
re-measurement is consistent with that entry rather than a new, separate
effect. **The gap between the two classifiers is unchanged inside noise.**
L1 was never expected to close it — it targeted a shared segmentation
mechanism, not the classifier-1 class-confusion behaviour identified in the
2026-09-26 decomposition — and it did not.

**Wall-time overhead did narrow, and that is worth flagging even though no
gate applies here.** Classifier 1's overhead over classifier 0 fell from
+16.7% (pre-L1) to +5.18% (post-L1). Read: fewer spurious lines/bands after
L1 means fewer lattice edges for the net's forward pass to score, which is
one plausible mechanism (not profiled, so a reading, not a finding). The
≤+25% wall gate belongs to step 5, which does not run because step 2 failed;
this number is informational only.

WER stayed better under classifier 1 both before and after L1 (−0.087 pp
pre-L1, −0.083 pp post-L1) — the same signature noted at decomposition:
more whole words come out right under the net, while the character-level
loss (CER, recall, precision, F1) concentrates.

## What this implies (for `ocrcer-linguist` / `ocrcer-architect`, not decided here)

- **No action is implied for `match.classifier`'s default.** It stays 0.
  This was never at risk of flipping on this measurement, and the gap that
  would need to close to put it at risk did not move.
- **L1's dense-table work should continue on its own track (W1, L2), not be
  treated as partial progress on the classifier-1 question.** The two are
  measurably independent: L1 moved both classifiers by about the same
  small amount and left their difference untouched.
- **The wall-time narrowing (+16.7% to +5.18%) is worth a dedicated look if
  the net is revisited**, since it suggests the net's cost scales with
  segmentation noise (spurious lines/bands) rather than being fixed per
  page — a lead for whoever next investigates `match.classifier=1`'s
  decoder interaction, not an action on its own.
- **The digit/uppercase confusion cluster remains the standing blocker** on
  any future attempt to ship the net, unchanged by two independent
  interventions now (L1, and the fusion/mode-2 attempt recorded on
  2026-09-26). Any next attempt needs a different lever: training data for
  dense small-font digit tables (`ocrcer-glyphs`), or a fix to the
  segmentation-vs-classification split responsible for Fate C in table
  rows (`ocrcer-runtime`) — not another decoder-weight or scale retune,
  which has now been tried twice without moving this cluster.
