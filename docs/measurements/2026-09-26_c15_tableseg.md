## Method

`ocr --layout` and `--raw` dumps for all 18 pages at engine defaults (mode 0).
A script (`classify_rows.py`, scratch-only, not committed) groups consecutive
band-fragments sharing `(baseline, fragment-count)` into "rows" (≥3
fragments — the structural signature of a table row spanning several
columns), then tags each row against four heuristics, manually spot-checked
against the raw `--layout` text and, for one case, the page's own pixels:

- **`rule_or_nontext`**: most fragments in the row have median glyph height
  ≤6px. Spot-checked (2 pages): confirmed as isolated single-component marks
  (width 3-4px) sitting inside or immediately adjacent to a real numeric
  column's x-range, decoding uniformly as a lone punctuation mark. These are
  thousands-separator marks in a numeric cell, detached from their digits and
  promoted to a spurious line/band-fragment of their own — a **line-finding**
  fault (category 2), not literally "a rule fused to a glyph" (category 4);
  reported under line finding with the mechanism named explicitly.
- **`line_shift(±N)`**: the row's first fragment's decoded text does not
  match the naive positional truth lookup at its own index, but matches one
  a small constant offset away. Spot-checked (2 pages) against the page's own
  truth line list and, in one case, the pixel bounding boxes: a two-line
  wrapped issuer/company name's continuation sits ~11-13px below the row
  band's main baseline and gets glued into the row's band-fragment sequence
  as a *later* fragment rather than being placed at its true reading-order
  position — a second, distinct **line-finding** fault, permanently shifting
  every later naive truth-index comparison on the page.
- **`xheight_lock`**: a flagged-descender line's `x_height` sits well above
  its row siblings' modal value with `source=Observed`. This is `diag-zero`'s
  known mechanism (per-line x-height voting locks onto the row's digit
  population). **X-height source** fault (category 3).
- **`word_oversplit`**: a fragment decodes as 2+ short (≤3-char) tokens at a
  `Valley`-sourced threshold. Spot-checked (1 page): a single 9-component,
  CUSIP-shaped identifier in one 40px-wide cell split into two short tokens
  by the gap-based word threshold — a **word-splitting** fault (category 5).
- **`unclassified`**: none of the above fire. Spot-checked (1 page): a
  9-member `FixedPitch` cell decoding as one well-formed identifier-shaped
  token, matching the cell's expected shape — i.e. **no segmentation anomaly
  detected**, not a fifth failure mode. Excluded from the cause tally below;
  any residual character-level miss on these rows would be a
  classification-stage (matching/net) question, out of this diagnosis's
  scope.

A row can trigger more than one heuristic. Per the assignment's "first stage
that goes wrong," rows are attributed to the earliest-failing pipeline stage
when more than one heuristic fires (line finding runs before x-height voting,
which runs before word splitting), so a row tagged both `line_shift` and
`word_oversplit` is counted once, under line finding.

631 rows classified across 17 of the 18 pages (`filing__s2__r000192` excluded
from row classification: `_c15_decomp.md` task 5 found it is not a dense
table page, unlike the other 17). Measured by script, spot-checked manually
on a total of 6 row instances across 4 pages — not a full manual audit of
all 631.

## Cause table (measured, heuristic classification + spot-check)

| Cause (category) | Rows | Share of classified |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2. Line finding (mark isolated as own line + row-band misordering) | 248 | 39.3% |
| 5. Word splitting (short identifier/abbreviation over-split) | 200 | 31.7% |
| 3. Per-line x-height source (`diag-zero` digit-band lock) | 70 | 11.1% |
| No segmentation anomaly detected (excluded from ranking) | 113 | 17.9% |
| 1. Binarisation, 4. rule/box fused to a glyph, 6. touching-digit interior cuts | 0 | — |

**Dominant cause: line finding (category 2), 248/631 rows (39.3% of
classified rows; 47.9% of the 518 rows where any anomaly was found).**
Two mechanisms, roughly 194 (mark-isolation) to 54 (row-band misordering) —
both upstream of word splitting and x-height voting, which is why they
compound with, rather than get masked by, the other two causes. Categories
1, 4 (literally: a rule fused to a glyph's own bounding box) and 6 did not
appear in this sample.

This corroborates `_c15_decomp.md`'s independent finding (Task 3: `,`↔digit
substitution pairs dominate the edit deltas) from a completely different
angle: mark-isolation directly explains why a thousands-separator comma is
the character most often lost, and word-oversplitting directly explains why
a short numeric run next to it gets torn into pieces.

Both classifiers decode from the same reconstructed lattice: line finding,
x-height voting, and word splitting all run once, upstream of and identical
for both `match.classifier=0` and `=1` — this diagnosis did not need a
separate mode-1 `--layout` pass to answer "under both classifiers," and the
`fatec` numbers below (independently decoded per mode) confirm the two
modes track each other within noise on this cohort, consistent with the
segmentation stage being the shared bottleneck.

(`table_gt_chars` rose from 10,380 to 11,822 under dcc=1 — the x-height
threshold reclassifies more lines as "table rows" once the fix changes their

of characters at dcc=0 and dcc=1; the "all rows" and CER lines are the

**`descender_cap_check=1` does not reduce Fate C or CER on these 18 pages —
every metric is flat to marginally worse.** This confirms the size-check
entry's own framing: `diag-zero`'s x-height-locking bug is real and present
(70 rows, 11.1%, in the cause table above) but is a minority contributor on
this specific dense-table cohort, not the lever that would close the gap.
Consistent with `xh-desc` being informational here, not a fold candidate for
this loss.

## Proposed minimal fix (description only — not applied on any branch)

For the dominant cause, two independent, additive changes to
`layout/lines.rs`'s band-fragment construction:

1. **Mark isolation**: a component far below the row's dominant/median
   member height (e.g. under ~40%) and narrow (single component, no
   siblings within its own height band) should attach to the nearest
   same-baseline-tolerance line rather than be promoted to a line of its
   own — extend the existing height-outlier handling in line grouping to
   merge rather than spawn.
2. **Row-band misordering**: when a band-fragment's own baseline differs
   from its sibling fragments' baseline by more than the row's own
   within-row tolerance (observed here: an 8-9px gap on a page whose normal
   line pitch is ~12-15px), it should not be glued into the current row's
   fragment sequence at all — it is a separate logical line and belongs at
   its own reading-order position, decided before word splitting runs.

For the second cause (word splitting): when a `Valley`-sourced split
produces two or more sub-words that are each short (≤3 chars) relative to
the whole component run in a compact span, prefer the single-word reading
unless a materially larger gap is present — `words::band_space_rules`
already carries a gap/valley computation this would gate on, not a new
signal.

Neither fix is applied here. This is a diagnosis; the fix is a design
decision for `ocrcer-architect` to weigh against the other loss sources this
chunk has already found.
