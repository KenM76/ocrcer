# Worst page named: r000583's 54.66% CER is serif-kerning bbox chaining, not order, splitting, or the two recent params

Diagnostic only. No param default, `params.tsv`, model, or `ARCHITECTURE.md` entry
was changed to produce these findings. All instrumentation was temporary,
gated behind `OCRCER_DEBUG_ATOMS` in `crates/ocrcer-core/src/layout/segment.rs`
(`atoms()` and `interior_cuts()`), and has been removed with `git checkout --`
after this document was written; `git status` and `git diff --stat` are clean
apart from this file. Builds used `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=target/runtime-diag`.
Every page was run individually, `--only filing__r000583` (or `--only
filing__r000572` for the one comparison page), one run at a time.

Every claim below is tagged **Observed** (read directly off instrumentation
or pixel data) or **Inferred** (reasoned from Observed evidence plus reading
the code, not separately measured).

---

## 1. Not a reading-order problem

**Observed**, control run (shipped defaults, `lines.underline_strip=1`,
`segment.split_min_x_heights=1.09`):

```
end-to-end   CER 54.661%  WER 84.663%  recall 15.337%  precision 15.823%  F1 15.576%
line-matched CER 54.661%  (reading-order-independent)
```

End-to-end and line-matched CER are bit-identical. `line-matched` pairs each
truth line to its best-matching read line before charging edit distance, so
a page read correctly but in the wrong row order would show a gap between
the two numbers; there is none. **Line grouping is also directly correct**:
`--layout` reports 11 lines against the truth's 11 lines, and each line's
`want` text (dumped by the bench tool from the truth JSON) lines up
one-to-one with the read line at the same index, in order — line 0 is truth
line 0 ("the offering and the expected closing date..."), line 10 is truth
line 10 ("written or oral..."), no swaps, no merges of separate text lines
(the underline-strip line-merging mechanism from
`docs/measurements/2026-09-23_underline_r000055_and_touching_r000022.md`
does not appear here — expected, since this page is plain prose with no
bordered boxes/rules for the strip to touch: see section 4).

## 2. Word splitting: a small, secondary loss, not the driver

**Observed**, summed over `--layout`'s per-line word counts: 158 words
produced against 163 truth words (`sum(len(line.split()) for line in
truth["lines"])`), a shortfall of 5 words (3.1%) spread across 4 of the 11
lines (line 0: −1, line 5: −1, line 8: −2, line 10: −1). Inspecting line 0's
decoded tokens against its 10 truth words shows the mechanism: `"of"` and
`"the"` (want positions 8–9) come out as one decoded token, `"orme"` — an
inter-word gap collapsed below the space threshold. This is the same
tight-set-text characteristic named in section 3, just occasionally crossing
a word boundary instead of staying inside one word. At 5 of 163 words, this
is not what produces a page at 54.66% CER.

## 3. The dominant mechanism: chained bounding-box overlap collapses whole words into oversized atoms that a fixed 3-cut budget and a vertical-only cut cannot recover

### 3.1 The scale of chaining, this page vs. a normal one

`layout/segment.rs`'s `atoms()` groups a word's components into one atom
whenever consecutive components' x-ranges overlap (`c.x0 < a.1`), by design,
for cases like an `i` and its dot or one ordinary kerned pair. **Observed**,
counting every such multi-member atom on each page:

| page | CER | words | multi-member atoms | atoms with ≥3 members | largest atom (members) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `filing__r000583` (worst) | 54.66% | 158 | 209 | **73** | **7** |
| `filing__r000572` (ordinary) | 4.91% | 790 | 330 | 3 | 3 |

`r000572` is dominated by the ordinary 2-member case (327 of 330 atoms) that
the rule exists for, and never chains past 3. `r000583` has **73** atoms
that chain 3 or more separate, pixel-disjoint components into a single atom
— up to **7** in one case — at a rate (0.46 such atoms per word vs. 0.004 on
`r000572`, roughly two orders of magnitude) that is specific to this page,
not a general property of the `filing` corpus.

### 3.2 A worked example: `"the"` becomes `"Øe"`

**Observed**, `word x=43..83` (line 0, want `"the"`): 2 atoms, `(43,67,
2 members)` and `(69,83, 1 member)`. The first atom's two members are
`t`: bbox `(43,53,33,52)` and `h`: bbox `(51,67,31,51)` — **two separate
connected components** (union-find labelling never merged them; this is not
touching ink, it is only their bounding boxes overlapping by 2px, x=51..53).
A raw-pixel ASCII crop of `x=43..68, y=28..54` (Sauvola-binarized page,
2x upscale by eye) shows why: `t`'s crossbar (row y=38) runs to x=52, and
`h`'s base serif (row y=50) starts at x=51 — **two different row-bands**,
12 rows apart, whose horizontal extents happen to overlap by 2 columns.
No vertical cut position separates them cleanly: a cut at x=51 leaves
`t`'s crossbar pixels at x=51–52 in the right (`h`) piece; a cut at x=53
leaves `h`'s serif pixels at x=51–52 in the left (`t`) piece. This is a
genuine geometric conflict of a serif-font kerning pair, not a filter gap —
column 51–52 carries real ink from *both* letters, just at different
heights.

`interior_cuts` still searches this 24px atom (`split_min=16.350` at this
line's x-height 15.0, so the 24px atom clears the gate) and returns
`cuts=[51,53,61]` — a genuine, non-degenerate valley search — but every
offered cut contaminates one of the two pieces as shown above. Decode's
chosen path takes the atom whole (`Single`, not `Split`) and matches it to
one bank prototype, producing `"Ø"` for the `t`+`h` pair; the trailing `e`
atom is separate and correctly recognized. Decoded line-0 tokens:
`["Øe","‰Ø","and","me","eæecæd","c/o™Ø","ææ","orme","‰Ø"]` against want
`["the","offering","and","the","expected","closing","date","of","the",
"offering."]` — every word with a crossbar/ascender-heavy letter run
(`t`, `f`, `h`, `l`, `k`) is damaged; `"and"` (no such letters, no bbox
chaining) comes out correct.

### 3.3 Why the multi-letter chains are worse than the 2-letter case

`"offering"` (line 0, want word 2) is `word x=93..196`, and its first atom
is `x=93..177` (84px) with **6 members** — o, f, f, e, r, i chained by
consecutive bbox overlaps of 1–6px each. `segment.max_splits = 3`
(`model/params.tsv`) is a fixed per-atom budget, independent of atom width
or member count: **Observed**, `interior_cuts` on this atom returns exactly
3 cuts (`[108,142,149]`), which can carve the 84px span into at most 4
pieces — enough for roughly 4 of the atom's 6 letters even in the best
case, and each offered cut carries the same column-ambiguity contamination
risk as section 3.2's 2-letter case. The atom decodes to 2 output tokens
(`"‰Ø"`) for what is 8 letters of `"offering"`. This is the aggregate
mechanism behind the page's top confusions (`t→""` 52 times, `r→""` 43,
`i→""` 28, `e→"æ"` 25, `o→""` 24, `f→""` 22, `l→""` 19 — deletions and
symbol substitutions concentrated on exactly the ascender/crossbar letters
that produce bbox overlap in serif type, `t h f l k b d`, while round or
non-extending letters — `a n d o s u` — that rarely chain stay largely
correct).

### 3.4 Named mechanism, with numbers

> `r000583` renders its prose in a serif face whose crossbar and base-serif
> strokes cause adjacent letters' bounding boxes to overlap by 1–6px even
> when the underlying ink is pixel-disjoint (73 atoms chain 3+ letters this
> way, up to 7, vs. 3 such atoms topping out at 3 on a 4.91%-CER comparison
> page — a two-orders-of-magnitude difference in rate). `atoms()`'s existing
> bbox-overlap rule, built for the ordinary i-dot/kerned-pair case, chains
> these into oversized atoms spanning most or all of a word. Two things then
> compound: `segment.max_splits = 3` is a fixed cut budget independent of
> atom width, so a 6–7-letter chained atom can be carved into at most 4
> pieces regardless; and because the serif overlap is a same-column,
> different-row conflict (crossbar top vs. base serif bottom), no single
> vertical cut position separates two overlapping letters without handing 1–3
> contaminating columns to one piece or the other. Decode ends up preferring
> the whole merged atom as a `Single` edge, matched to whatever bank
> prototype scores best against the fused shape — nonsense symbols
> (`Ø`, `æ`, `™`) or nothing at all. This lands almost entirely on
> ascender/crossbar letters (`t h f l k b d`, the top confusions are
> exactly these deleted or mismatched), while letters that don't produce
> bbox overlap (`a n d o s u`) decode correctly, which is why whole simple
> words like `"and"` survive untouched next to badly damaged neighbours on
> the same line.

## 4. The two recent shipped changes: negligible here

Both toggled from the diagnostic binary, `--only filing__r000583`, nothing
else changed:

| condition | end-to-end CER | line-matched CER |
|---|---|---|
| shipped control (`underline_strip=1`, `split_min=1.09`) | 54.661% | 54.661% |
| `lines.underline_strip=0` | 54.661% (bit-identical) | 54.661% |
| `segment.split_min_x_heights=1.15` (pre-chunk value) | 53.756% | 53.756% |
| both reverted together | 53.756% | 53.756% |

- **Underline strip contributes exactly 0.000pp** on this page — expected,
  since this is unbroken prose with no bordered number boxes or rules for the
  strip to touch (the mechanism the strip targets and the mechanism named in
  section 3 do not overlap on this page at all).
- **The split-gate move (1.15→1.09) costs 0.905pp** on this page specifically
  (54.661% vs. 53.756% at the old, looser gate) — a small, second-order
  effect in the opposite direction from the corpus-wide gain that change was
  measured to produce (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s 2026-09-23 "Split gate"
  entry: finfilings CER 17.064→16.932 corpus-wide). On this page, offering
  more narrow-atom splits at the tighter 1.09 gate does not help against a
  page already dominated by 3+-letter chained atoms, and evidently loses a
  handful of characters net. Neither change is within two orders of
  magnitude of the ~54% mechanism in section 3.

## 5. Named mechanism, in one line

> Not reading order, not word splitting, not the underline strip, and only
> marginally the split-gate move: `r000583`'s 54.66% CER is dominated by
> serif-kerning bounding-box chains — up to 7 pixel-disjoint letters glued
> into one oversized atom by the existing i-dot/kerned-pair bbox-overlap
> rule, at a rate ~100x a normal page — which a fixed 3-cut-per-atom budget
> and a vertical-only cut cannot resolve without contaminating one piece or
> the other, landing damage almost entirely on ascender/crossbar letters
> (`t h f l k b d`) while non-overlapping letters decode correctly on the
> same line.

when the env var is unset.
