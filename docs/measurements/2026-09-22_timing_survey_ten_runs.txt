Timing survey -- every head-to-head run recorded to date
========================================================
Compiled 2026-09-22. NOT a timing benchmark: see "What this is not" below.

Method
------
Each head-to-head run in docs/measurements/ prints a "seconds" row for both
engines. Nobody had read that column. This file collects all ten, divides by
the run's own page count, and groups by bank size. No new run was made; every
number below is lifted from a file already in this directory, named in the
last column.

Two findings
------------
1. THE PER-PAGE PROJECTION HOLDS. Section 4.1 projected "comfortably under a
   second per page single-threaded". Observed range 0.26 to 0.49 s/page across
   every bank and corpus tried. Confirmed as far as uncontrolled wall clock
   can confirm anything.

2. THE COMPARATIVE PROJECTION DOES NOT. Section 4.1 also said "faster than the
   neural design it replaces rather than slower". All three runs at the
   14,832-prototype bank are faster; one of seven at a larger bank is.

   Mean OCRcer seconds per page, grouped by bank:

       14,832 prototypes    0.305    (n=3)
       17,610 prototypes    0.444    (n=1)
       18,540 prototypes    0.401    (n=5)
       22,248 prototypes    0.495    (n=1)

   A bank 1.50x larger (14,832 -> 22,248) costs 1.62x per page. That is close
   to linear, which is what matching dominating the page looks like.

   ocrs over the same ten runs: 0.35 to 0.42 s/page, no trend against bank
   size, because nothing about the bank reaches it. A fixed network costs what
   it costs.

   The 17,610 row sits at 0.444 where the trend wants about 0.38. It is the
   only run with known unrelated load on the machine. It is left where it fell
   rather than dropped.

The consequence worth carrying forward
--------------------------------------
Bank growth is not free. Every charset class, every face added for coverage,
every scale added to the ladder buys accuracy with page time on a near-linear
curve, and ocrs pays nothing for any of it. ARCHITECTURE.md section 9 argued
the prototype budget on file size and build time -- both trivial, 1.5 MB and
45 s. The cost that actually binds is per-page matching, and it had not been
priced anywhere.

What this is not
----------------
NOT a controlled timing measurement, and no figure here should be quoted as
one.
  - The runs are sequential on a machine carrying varying unrelated load. Run
    I is known to have had a compile running alongside it.
  - The harness wall clock includes page rendering and ground-truth scoring
    alongside recognition, on both sides, in unknown proportion.
  - The two columns within a run are not perfectly paired: the engines run one
    after the other, not interleaved.
  - OCRcer was handed segmentation, spacing and layout from ground truth in
    every run. ocrs did all three itself. Whatever chunks 2 and 6 cost is
    added to the OCRcer column and to nothing else.

So: the per-bank TREND is the robust part, holding across four bank sizes and
three corpora. Any single row's margin is not.

Open item for ocrcer-bench
--------------------------
A timing mode that measures recognition alone, on an idle machine, rendering
and scoring excluded, both engines given the same pages in the same order --
and once chunks 2 and 6 exist, end to end on both sides, which is the only
comparison that answers the question section 4.1 asked.
