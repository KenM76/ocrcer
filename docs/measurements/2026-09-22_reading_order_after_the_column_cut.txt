A banded row came back right-hand-fragment first, and only CER could see it
================================================================================

2026-09-22.  bench/pages-cov, all 625 pages, stride 1.  Model
model/out/ocrcer.ocrw t=2026-09-22 12:10:54.  words.min_valley_x_heights at the
shipped 0.3 throughout.  Before: target/release/ocr.exe built 12:19:24.  After:
the same source plus one change, built 13:27:48.

WHAT WAS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reading order was decided after the column cut, by sorting the finished lines
on their top edge:

    lines.sort_by(|a, b| a.y0.cmp(&b.y0).then(a.x0.cmp(&b.x0)).then(...));

Two fragments of one banded row do not share a top edge.  Whichever fragment
holds the taller glyph -- a digit, a capital, an ascender -- starts higher, and
the sort then puts it first regardless of where it sits across the page.  On a
label-and-value row, which is what an invoice, a statement and a receipt are
made of, that returns the value before its own label.

Caught on a photographed receipt.  Truth reads

    DATE : 15/01/2019 11:05:16 AM

and the engine returned two lines, in this order:

    ; w01/2019 11:0516 AM
    Date

With --set lines.column_gap_heights=0 the same page returns one line in the
right order, which localises it to the cut's fragments rather than to
recognition.  The layout dump, once it printed each line's x extent, showed the
two pieces at x 30..63 and x 124..324 -- emitted widest-first.

THE FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sort the *bands*, while a row is still one object, then cut each left to right.
Fragments then leave the stage adjacent and in order and there is nothing to
sort afterwards.  Three lines moved; no other behaviour was touched.

For a row that is never cut the two orderings are identical -- the band's
bounding box is the line's, and both sorts are stable -- so the change is
provably a no-op wherever the cut does not fire.  That is the control below.

WHAT THAT TABLE ESTABLISHES, AND WHY THE SHAPE MATTERS MORE THAN THE SIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The control holds.  At cut off, five metrics are identical to five decimals.
   The binary provably differs -- it prints a field the old one did not -- so
   this is a measured no-op and not an unrebuilt binary.  (It was an unrebuilt
   binary the first time; see the note at the end.)

2. Every order-insensitive metric is untouched at every arm.  Word recall,
   precision and F1 do not move by a digit anywhere.  That is the signature of
   a pure reordering: the same words were found, in the same numbers, on the
   same pages.

3. Every order-sensitive metric improves wherever the cut fires, by 0.4 to 8.8
   CER points.  The worst arm is the one where the cut fires most.

So the defect was invisible to precisely the metric most often quoted.  A
bag-of-words F1 cannot see a row read backwards; it was 74.234% before and
74.234% after.  Had the layout been judged on word F1 alone -- which is the
natural thing to do when the question is "did the cut split the right places"
-- this would have gone unnoticed indefinitely.

The corollary is worth keeping: a metric that is invariant under an error class
is not evidence about that error class.  Reading order needs CER or an explicit
order check, and nothing else in the suite will catch it.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cut is still a net loss on this corpus.  At the shipped 2.5 it costs 1.598
CER points and 1.789 word F1 against leaving it off, down from 3.359 and 1.789
before the fix -- so the fix halved the character cost and left the word cost
exactly where it was.  Choosing the parameter is a separate question and is not
settled here.

These are rendered pages.  The 29-scan corpus is where the cut and the
synthetic corpus have disagreed before, and a scan's gaps carry the additional
spread of its own binarisation.

A NOTE ON THE FIRST RUN OF THIS COMPARISON, WHICH WAS FALSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first "after" sweep returned byte-identical numbers at every arm and was
nearly recorded as "the fix changes nothing on rendered pages."  The binary had
not been rebuilt: `ocr` is declared with required-features = ["pages"], which
was not in default, so `cargo build --release` skipped it silently while its
crate printed "Compiling".

Identity with the previous run is exactly what an unrebuilt binary produces,
and it is also exactly what the no-op control was designed to look like.  The
two are indistinguishable from the numbers alone.  What settled it was making
the binary emit something new -- one extra field in the layout dump -- and
looking for it.

`pages` is now in `default` so a plain build builds the measurement binaries.
