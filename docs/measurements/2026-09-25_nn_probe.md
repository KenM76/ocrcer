# 2026-09-25: throwaway neural probe (chunk-15 pre-check)

Per `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §11, "Candidate: a throwaway neural probe before
chunk 15, on the extractor's own dumps." Branch `nn-probe`, not merged.
Every number below is **measured** (a script run, logged); none is fitted
into the model, and nothing here touches `model/out` in the main tree or
the score set (finfilings, finfilings-val, pages-cov, fixtures were never
read).

## Headline

**The prototype matcher still wins.** On the real-crop sample: matcher
92.37%, network-with-geometry 91.13%, network-no-geometry 86.76%
(char top-1, exact match). The 107-dim geometry features help the network
(+4.4pp over the no-geometry ablation) but do not close the gap to the
matcher already shipping in `model/out/ocrcer.ocrw`. This is a loss for the
probe's premise, stated as prominently as the win below.

**The win**: geometry features are clearly load-bearing for the network —
without them, punctuation collapses to 56% (vs 86% with geometry, vs 98%
for the matcher) and case pairs (`0/O/o`) do relatively better only because
the network without shape cues falls back on frequency priors. This
corroborates `CLAUDE.md`'s baseline-relative feature-group argument
independent of the matcher path.

## Method (how obtained)

- Rust: `crates/ocrcer-bench/src/bin/nn_dump.rs` (new bin, `nn-dump`,
  `ocrcer-bench`). Calls `ocrcer_core::feature::extract_with_grid` (new,
  small addition to `ocrcer-core/src/feature.rs`: shares its inner
  computation with the existing `extract()`, adds the 32x32 grid as a
  second return value) — the same extractor the bank build and the runtime
  matcher use. No feature computation happens in Python.
- Bank renders: `ocrcer_build::bank::{Fonts, Renderer}` against the
  54 shippable faces (`model/fonts.tsv`, `include_local_only=false`), all
  187 charset classes, sizes 16/20/24/32/48px, 4 deterministic-seeded
  variants each (0=clean, 1-3=seeded scan damage: sub-pixel shift, partial
  blur, additive noise, threshold jitter, 3x3 erode/dilate). Split
  80/20 train/val by a seeded hash of (class, face, size, variant), not by
  page or font family, so no leakage between the two.
  - `bank_train`: 160,099 rows. `bank_val`: 40,281 rows.
- Real crops: `D:/Dev/ExcludedPrivate/ocrcer/pages/finfilings-train`,
  gated on `bench/splits/manifest.tsv`'s `multifinben-englishocr` /
  `train` rows only (`splits::assert_fittable` re-checked per row; never
  finfilings, finfilings-val, or pages-cov). The manifest's 427 train rows
  are grouped by shard with an uneven shard-size distribution (shard 1
  alone holds 131/427); reading a prefix would have biased the sample
  toward one shard, so `nn-dump` took every 5th manifest row instead
  (`stride=5`), yielding **86 pages spread proportionally across all 8
  shards** (4/26/13/4/5/13/7/14 per shard) rather than the full 427 —
  a wall-time bound (measured ~18.5s/page under observed machine
  contention; full 427 pages projected ~2.5-3h), reported here rather than
  run silently. `page_stride: 5` is recorded in `summary.json`.
  - A word only contributes chars if OCRcer's own segmentation produced
    exactly the truth word's character count (same gate at the line
    level for word count). This is a **selection-bias filter that favours
    already-well-segmented text**, not a random sample of the real pages:
    - lines qualifying: 7,657 / 11,195 (68.4%)
    - words qualifying: 13,087 / 15,636 (83.7%)
    - chars dumped: 72,874; chars out of charset: 0; chars with a bad crop
      rect: 0
  - For each qualifying real char, `nn-dump` also runs
    `ocrcer_core::r#match::nearest(model, &raw, k=1, italic_ok=true)` using
    the fitted `model/out/ocrcer.ocrw` (rebuilt in this worktree before the
    dump), so the matcher and the two network variants are compared on
    **identical crops** with identical features.
- Python: `tools/nnprobe/train.py`. Computes nothing from pixels — loads
  the Rust-dumped `G`/`X`/`y` arrays and the matcher's own dumped `top1`
  column. Architecture per the §11 contract: conv3x3x16-relu-pool ->
  conv3x3x32-relu-pool -> dense(2048+107 -> 128)-relu -> dense(-> 187).
  Ablation: `no_geometry` drops the 107-dim concat at the dense head.
  `--wide` variant not run (not needed to answer the headline question).
  Seeded (`SEED=20260925` for Python/NumPy/PyTorch). Adam, lr=1e-3,
  batch 256, 12 epochs.

## Conclusion

The throwaway probe answers the question it was built to ask: on this
project's own charset, fonts, and real filing crops, a small CNN with the
extractor's 107-dim features concatenated in gets close to (but does not
beat) the already-shipping prototype matcher, and loses badly to it once
the geometry features are removed. Chunk 15 should not expect a free
accuracy win from replacing the matcher with a network of this size and
this training set; if a network is pursued it should be additive
(ensemble/fallback on low-confidence matcher decisions) rather than a
replacement, and geometry features are not optional input to it.

- Line qualify rate: 68.4% (7,657/11,195) — lines where OCRcer's line

- Word qualify rate: 83.7% (13,087/15,636) — of qualifying lines, words

### Item 1 — was round 1 undertrained, or is ~85% a shared ceiling?

Round 1 trained a fixed 12 epochs. Round 2 trained to convergence with
`Adam` + `StepLR` (halving every 15 epochs) and early stopping (patience 10
epochs on render-val accuracy, best checkpoint restored):

| variant | epochs run | best epoch | best render-val acc | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| with_geometry | 26 (early-stopped) | 16 | **0.8512** | 152.0 s |
| no_geometry | 27 (early-stopped) | 17 | **0.7744** | 155.3 s |

Round 1's fixed-12-epoch numbers were 0.8433 (with-geometry) and 0.7649
(no-geometry, both from `docs/measurements/2026-09-25_nn_probe.md` round-1
section). Full convergence adds only **+0.79 pp** and **+0.95 pp**
respectively — round 1 was already close to converged. **Undertraining is
ruled out as the explanation for the ~85% ceiling.**

The second half of item 1 was to check whether the matcher shares that
ceiling (which would point at "glyph twins" — í/i, curly-quote pairs — as
an inherent limit of the damaged-render distribution, not a network
weakness). It does not: the matcher's own top-1 on the identical render-val
set, computed via `ocrcer_core::match::nearest` inside `nn_dump.rs`, is
**60.02%** (0 no-match rows), 25 points below the converged network. So the
~85% figure is not a shared classifier ceiling on this distribution — the
network handles this synthetic bank-render/damage mix measurably better
than the fitted matcher does. Read this narrowly: the matcher's distance
weights and gating were fitted against real scan behaviour (chunk 12b), not
against this synthetic damage model, so a large matcher/network gap here is
plausible and this number should not be read as "the network beats the
matcher" — see item 2 below for the comparison that actually matters (real
crops), which reverses which side gets the *benefit* of that gap.

### Item 2 — real crops in training: page-disjoint fold cross-validation

86 real pages, page-disjoint 43/43 split, `random.Random(seed=20260925)`
shuffle then half-split (`fold_split_stems`). Two folds, each training on
bank renders + one real half and testing on the other real half (net and
matcher scored on the *identical* held-out crops):

| fold | train (bank+real) n | test n | net acc | matcher acc (same test rows) | net wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fold1 (train=A, test=B) | 198,540 | 34,433 | **0.9755** | 0.9176 | 190.0 s |
| fold2 (train=B, test=A) | 194,532 | 38,441 | **0.9752** | 0.9293 | 189.5 s |
| **mean** | — | — | **0.9753** | **0.9234** | — |

**This reverses round 1's headline.** Round 1 trained on renders only and
found the matcher ahead (92.37% vs. 91.13%). Once real training crops are
included — which is chunk 15's actual contract, not renders-only — the
network beats the matcher by **+5.2 pp mean** on held-out real pages from
the same corpus. The round-1 conclusion ("the network loses to the
already-shipping matcher") was an artifact of the renders-only training
condition it tested, not a durable property of network-vs-matcher on this
task.

Caveats, stated as prominently as the win: this is still the same
selection-biased 86-page/427-page slice as round 1 (68.4% line-qualify,
83.7% word-qualify — see the repeated section below), on one architecture
and one training recipe, with no hyperparameter search. It says "a small
CNN trained with real crops can beat the matcher on this slice," not "the
matcher should be replaced" — chunk 15's own scoring gates (finfilings,
pages-cov) were correctly never touched by this probe and remain the actual
bar.

### Item 3 — fusion: this is a loss, reported as one

Extended `nn_dump.rs` to also emit the matcher's top-5 classes and
distances per real crop (`Sink5`, `real_topk_class.u16`, `real_topk_dist.f32`).
The "simple fusion" specified: fit an affine map from the network's
`-log softmax(p)` to the matcher's distance units via OLS on one fold's
test rows (`np.linalg.lstsq`), apply the mapped threshold to pick net vs.
matcher on the other fold's test rows.

| | scale_a | scale_b | fusion acc | net acc | matcher acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fusion1 (scale fit on fold2 test = real-A, applied to fold1 test = real-B) | -0.2265 | 4.4072 | 0.9178 | 0.9755 | 0.9176 |
| fusion2 (scale fit on fold1 test = real-B, applied to fold2 test = real-A) | -1.3216 | 4.3454 | 0.9286 | 0.9752 | 0.9293 |
| mean | — | — | **0.9232** | **0.9753** | **0.9234** |

**The fusion does not work.** Fusion accuracy tracks the matcher's accuracy
almost exactly (0.9178 vs. 0.9176; 0.9286 vs. 0.9293) and is ~5.2 pp below
the network alone. `scale_a` fits **negative** in both directions, which is
the wrong sign: a larger `-log p` (a less-confident network prediction)
should map to a *larger* distance-equivalent (justifying more deference to
the matcher), not a smaller one. A negative slope means the fused threshold
inverts that relationship, and the fusion rule ends up deferring to the
matcher on the large majority of rows almost regardless of net confidence —
which is why the result tracks the matcher, not some useful blend. Per-group
evidence for this: on `lowercase` in fold1 the fusion is *below both*
standalone classifiers (11,674 correct vs. matcher's 11,709 and net's
11,898) — proof the naive scale-fit is occasionally worse than either input
alone, not just "matcher-shaped."

The oracle numbers explain why there is not much for a *better* fusion to
recover, and this is worth reading before concluding fusion is the right
next investment: `matcher_wrong_net_right_frac` is 6.34% (fold1) / 5.64%
(fold2) — the net rescues most of what the matcher gets wrong — but
`net_wrong_matcher_right_frac` is only 0.55% (fold1) / 1.04% (fold2): the
matcher only rarely gets right what the net gets wrong. Oracle-union
accuracy is 0.9810 / 0.9856, only ~0.5–1.0 pp above net alone (0.9755 /
0.9752). **Given real training data, the practical recommendation is to
prefer the network over this fusion scheme, not to invest further in this
particular linear-scale fusion** — the OLS-fit conversion as specified in
the task does not produce a useful decision rule, and a better one (e.g. a
constrained-positive-slope or isotonic calibration, or simply "trust net
unless its own confidence is very low") was out of scope for this round and
is left as an open item, not implemented and not claimed as working.

### Round 2 conclusion

Round 1's "matcher wins" verdict does not survive item 2: it was specific
to the renders-only training condition, and reverses (network +5.2 pp mean)
once real crops are added, which is what chunk 15 will actually do. Item 1
rules out undertraining and shows the render-val ceiling is not a shared
matcher/network limit. Item 3 is a clear loss: the specified linear fusion
does not work and should not be carried forward as-is. Item 4 confirms and
sharpens round 1's worst finding (0/O/o) down to root cause: it's a
digit-vs-letter problem, not a case problem, and it's specific to the digit
`0` (53% own-class accuracy) rather than to `O`/`o` (81%/94%). Net effect
on the chunk 15 recommendation: a network trained with real crops looks
more promising than round 1 suggested, but only in the pure top-1 sense
measured here, on this selection-biased slice, without the fusion, gating,
or calibration machinery the matcher already has in production — none of
that is re-derived, re-scored, or a substitute for chunk 15's own gates.
