# Chunk 15 size-bucketed diagnostic — the pre-decided branch check

Runs the one diagnostic `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §11's "Chunk 15 decomposition"
entry pre-registers, with both outcome branches decided in advance:

> **If the net trails the matcher on small aligned crops:** the net lacks
> small-size and dense-digit coverage. The route is a retrain.
> **If the net matches or beats the matcher on those aligned crops, and the
> loss sits in Fate C (merged or split digits):** the route is training junk
> on touching-digit merges, or segmentation work on dense tables.

Diagnosis only. No parameter, weight, or default was written or changed.
`finfilings`, `finfilings-val`, `pages-cov`, any fixture, and `bench/ident`
were never touched. No page text appears in this document or in any commit
on this branch.

## Part 1 — per-crop top-1 accuracy, bucketed by x-height

Methodology: `gen15_probe.rs`'s alignment gate, unchanged — a truth line
pairs with a decoded line by index only when word counts match; a truth word
pairs with a decoded word only when character counts match; each qualifying
character's crop is rebuilt via `segment::build_with`/`segment::crop` and
accepted only on an exact box match against the decoded `CharBox.rect`. One
decode per page at engine defaults (`match.classifier=0`); both classifiers
are then read off the *same* extracted crop, so accuracy is never compared
across two different inputs. This is why the qualifying population is a
minority of every page's characters — the gate selects for already-cleanly-
segmented characters, the same known bias `2026-09-26_c15_generalisation.md`
documents.

Bucket edges are the crop's line `x_height` in pixels
(`lines::TextLine.x_height`), equal-frequency over the distinct observed
values, each bucket holding at least 300 crops (the trailing under-sized
remainder folded into the bucket before it). Edges are the actual observed
values, stated below — nothing rounded to a round number.

in every bucket, but modestly (not the scale of a coverage gap that would
explain a 20-point page-level CER delta on its own).

## Part 2 — Fate-C rate on table rows, sample (a), both modes

"Table row" is a structural proxy, not a semantic label: a line whose
reconstructed `x_height` is at or below **9.000px**, the upper edge of
sample (b)'s smallest bucket (chosen from the larger, more representative
sample so the threshold isn't picked from the same 18 pages it's applied
to). This is inferred from geometry, not read off page content — it is not
a "this is a holdings table" ground truth, and is stated as a proxy for
exactly that reason.

Fate C, `c15_trace_probe.rs`'s definition, reused verbatim: the decoded
edge does not correspond 1:1 to its ground-truth token. **Deliberate
departure from that probe's own corpus-wide convention:** the original code
silently drops a whole line from every fate bucket when its word count
doesn't match the truth's word count (`continue`, no accounting at all).
That is a survivorship-bias risk specifically for the population under
study here — dense table rows are exactly the lines most likely to fail
that coarse check first, before any per-word accounting even starts. This
run instead counts a whole-line mismatch as **100% Fate C, over that
line's own ground-truth character count** — the same attribution the
original probe already uses for a within-line word mismatch, just no
longer silently exempting the whole-line case. This is a stated,
deliberate methodological change, not a silent one; it is why the numbers
below read very differently from the corpus-wide 16.5%/15.9% baseline in
`2026-09-26_c15_trace.md`, and that is the point of the change, not an
error.

| mode | table-row (x-height ≤ 9px) Fate C | all-page Fate C |
|---|---|---|
| mode 0 (matcher, defaults) | 9,550 / 10,380 = **92.00%** | 38,176 / 46,722 = **81.71%** |
| mode 1 (net, `nn.scale=√2`) | 9,541 / 10,380 = **91.92%** | 38,150 / 46,722 = **81.65%** |

Two things stand out:

1. **On these 18 pages specifically, Fate C is enormous** — 82–92% of
   ground-truth characters never even reach a fair 1:1 aligned comparison,
   versus 16.5%/15.9% corpus-wide. That gap between "these 18 pages" and
   "the whole corpus" is the survivorship-bias effect the accounting fix
   was built to surface: these are exactly the pages where segmentation
   most often fails the coarse line-level check first.
2. **Fate C is nearly identical between mode 0 and mode 1** — a spread of
   0.08pp (table rows) and 0.06pp (whole page). Whichever classifier is
   driving the decode, the lattice's chosen cut fails to land on the
   ground-truth boundary at essentially the same rate. This is not a
   difference the classifier choice is causing.

Because Fate C swamps the aligned population 4–14× over on these pages
(only 8.0–8.1% of table-row characters, 18.3–18.4% of all characters,
ever reach a fair aligned comparison) and is shared almost exactly between
modes, the majority driver of the CER loss on the loss-concentrated pages
is segmentation/word-boundary breakdown common to both engines — not a
difference in classification accuracy between them.

## Which pre-decided branch the data selects

**Neither branch fits cleanly; the data leans toward Branch B's diagnosis,
while Branch A's literal trigger is technically true but weak.**

- Branch A's stated trigger ("the net trails the matcher on small aligned
  crops") is literally satisfied — Part 1, sample (a), shows the net
  trailing in all five buckets. But the margin is modest (0.9–5.6pp, not a
  scale that explains a 20-point single-page CER delta) and is not reliably
  digit-specific: the net wins on digits in the smallest bucket, ties in
  another, and the one bucket with a large digit gap has n=44 — real, but
  a narrow slice. Sample (b) shows the opposite pattern at the corpus
  scale: the net does not lack small-size or dense-digit coverage there —
  it wins in 9 of 12 buckets, decisively in the single largest bucket
  (15–16px, n=24,767, +9.3pp all / +11.7pp digit). A retrain aimed at
  "small-size and dense-digit coverage" (Branch A's remedy) would be
  chasing a gap that mostly is not present in the general population and is
  modest even where it is present.
- Branch B's trigger ("the net matches or beats the matcher on aligned
  crops, and the loss sits in Fate C") is not strictly met either — sample
  (a)'s aligned crops show the net trailing, not matching or beating. But
  its underlying diagnosis is exactly what the Fate-C numbers support: on
  the loss-driving pages, 82–92% of characters never reach a fair aligned
  comparison at all, and that rate is shared almost exactly (<0.1pp
  spread) between the two classifiers. The classifiers are not
  meaningfully different where it matters most on these pages, because
  neither one gets a fair shot at the majority of the characters there.

**Practical translation.** The evidence does not support a retrain-first
route: the aligned-crop gap that motivates it is modest, inconsistent on
digits, and largely absent in the general (sample-b) population — a
retrain would improve a minority of characters (the ~18% of this sample's
characters that ever reach a fair aligned comparison) while leaving the
majority driver untouched. The Fate-C numbers point at segmentation on
dense small-font numeric tables as the larger lever: word/line boundaries
need to land on the ground-truth cut before either classifier's accuracy
can matter on these pages. That is `ocrcer-runtime`'s territory
(binarization, morphological repair, touching-character separation, per
`docs/PLAN.md` §4's risk-mitigation ladder), not `ocrcer-glyphs`'s or
`ocrcer-linguist`'s. If a retrain is still pursued for other reasons, this
data says not to expect it to close the mode-1 loss on these 18 pages —
that loss is downstream of where the lattice cuts the page, not of which
classifier reads the resulting crop. Whether this implies a segmentation
or binarization change is `ocrcer-architect`'s call to make, not this
report's; this report says only where the evidence points.

## Honesty notes

- Sample (a)'s digit subgroups at n=23 and n=19 are flagged as noise and
  not read as a real per-bucket digit effect, per the corpus's own
  convention for small subgroups.
- The "table row" x-height threshold (9.000px) is a structural proxy
  inferred from geometry, never checked against actual page content — it
  is not a semantic "this is a table" label, and is stated as a proxy for
  that reason.
- Part 1 and Part 2 use different alignment populations by construction:
  Part 1 decodes each page once (engine defaults) and reads both
  classifiers off the same crop; Part 2 decodes each page independently
  under each mode's own engine (so its own lattice can differ). Their
  respective "qualifying fraction" numbers are not the same measurement
  and should not be read against each other directly.
- The Fate-C numbers in this document are not comparable to
  `2026-09-26_c15_trace.md`'s corpus-wide 16.5%/15.9% without the stated
  accounting change in mind — the two numbers measure different things
  (whole-corpus average under a convention that drops whole-line
  mismatches, versus an 18-page loss-concentrated subset under a
  convention that counts them). The gap between them is the intended
  effect of fixing that accounting gap, not a contradiction.
