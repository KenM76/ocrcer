--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Headline finding: this is NOT where the t/f/i/l/r/o deletions come from
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Working hypothesis going in (round2(a)): fused atoms formed by `atoms()`'s
bbox-merge step survive with a column profile whose true between-letter
minimum is rejected by `interior_cuts`' margin or ceiling test, so the
Viterbi decoder never sees a split point and deletes a letter.

Measured: across 352 traced atoms (every atom on the page with width >=
1.0x-height, plus all 54 bbox-merged "fused" atoms regardless of width)
and 20 of them visually confirmed against crops, cut-candidate generation
is doing close to the right thing almost everywhere it was checked. Only
one atom on the entire page is even an ambiguous near-miss, and even that
one's available local minimum is not the boundary that would need to be
cut. The 130+ t/f/i/l/r/o deletions in this page's confusion table
(t:47, f:18, i:18, l:18, r:17, o:12) are not explained by this mechanism.
This directly narrows, rather than confirms, round2(a)'s hypothesis for
this specific page.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Recommendation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not relax `valley_fraction` (ceiling test). No sampled evidence
supports it, and every zero-kept atom directly inspected (10 of them,
spanning the fused and single-member populations) was a *correct*
rejection -- loosening the ceiling would newly admit the g/d/x/n-neck
false triggers found here as real cut candidates, degrading atoms that
currently work, for a benefit that in this sample is zero.

Do not prioritize adding a contour/drop-fall candidate source on the
strength of this page. The "no vertical-projection minimum exists at all"
failure mode that would motivate it is essentially absent here: 1 atom
out of 352+54 traced had no raw local minimum, and that one atom (x=
608..626, "d") was also confirmed to be a single glyph that correctly
needs no split. A drop-fall/contour source has no evidence of payoff on
r000583 specifically (it may still be justified on other pages or other
failure classes -- this measurement only speaks to this page).

Escalate to ocrcer-architect: the t/f/i/l/r/o deletion pattern driving
r000583's 40.633% CER is not explained by segment.rs's cut-candidate
generation. Of 54 bbox-merged "fused" atoms (round2(a)'s named suspect
population) only one is even an ambiguous near-miss, and its own
available local minimum is the wrong one to admit. The next diagnostic
step should move upstream of segment.rs:
  - components.rs / binarize.rs: check whether t/f/i/l/r/o strokes are
    surviving as intact connected components at all on this page (a
    thin-stroke dropout, anti-aliasing threshold effect, or a
    component-size filter could delete these specific letters before
    `atoms()` ever sees them -- consistent with narrow-stroke serif
    letters being exactly the ones in the confusion table).
  - match.rs: check whether correctly-segmented single-letter atoms for
    these letters are being pruned or mis-scored by the hole-count/aspect
    prune ahead of full distance matching, since t/f/i/l are all
    low-hole-count, narrow-aspect glyphs that would land in a
    heavily-populated prune bucket.
This measurement rules out (rather than confirms) one candidate
mechanism; it does not identify the real one, which is outside this
task's scope (cut-candidate generation specifically).
